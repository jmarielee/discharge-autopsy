WHY                The artifact class is the written material a patient is holding
                   after an encounter. This specimen carries no encounter.
                   Anchor, front matter: "Talk with the hospital staff about each
                   of the items that are listed in the guide." The document names
                   itself as an instrument completed during an encounter, not one
                   generated at its conclusion. Every content field is unpopulated:
                   no name, no medical problem, no medicine name, no dose, no
                   appointment, no phone number.
                   Four of six taxonomy classes cannot be evaluated against it.
                   P1 requires two instructions sharing an observable token — the
                   set contains no instructions. P2 requires a conditional
                   instruction to act; the only conditionals are unfilled stems
                   ("If I need medicine for a headache") with empty amount and
                   route cells. P3 requires a documented clinical state to conflict
                   with a handout's scope — no state is recorded anywhere. P4
                   requires a structured field asserting absence alongside a
                   narrative asserting presence — there is no narrative.
                   The two classes that remain mechanically evaluable, P5 and P6,

                   "If I have a serious health problem, I should call:" and its
                   unfilled "Phone number:". That firing would register the
                   specimen's pre-issuance state, not a property a reader
                   encounters. A blank the form supplies for completion is not the
                   same object as a required field empty at issue, and the
                   detection signature cannot separate them.
                   Even admitted on the permissive reading, the threshold in
                   rules.md §4 refuses: dozens of anchored candidates, none at P3
                   or higher.
